# Combat readability: evidence and validation

Research checked 22 November 2026. The problem is not just “make damage flash”: a player needs to identify the enemy and its likely action, recognize the actual wind-up and affected area, and then connect a real health loss with its source. This is an improvement hypothesis, not a claim that the issue is solved or that retention increased.

## What other developers report

- In Motion Twin's *Dead Cells* art-design discussion, threats and projectiles receive high saturation, contrast and brightness relative to subdued backgrounds, so fast players can distinguish immediate danger. That is the developers' design rationale, not a measured result for SPLATR. [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-giving-back-colors-to-cryptic-worlds-in-i-dead-cells-i-)
- Poki published developer-reported results for *Smash Room*: randomized first objects retained 33.7% (glass), 27.4% (cake), and 23.8% (phone) of players at three minutes, a 9.9 percentage-point gap between glass and phone. The article does not publish the sample size or uncertainty. The experiment concerns opening objects, not enemy damage. In *Satisbox Mini Games*, the developer reordered mini-games after reviewing progression; average session time was reported as 4:50, then 5:43, then 6:23. These sequential updates do not isolate a single cause or predict SPLATR's results. [Poki developer case studies](https://poki.com/blog/game-events-new-tool-for-understanding-your-players)
- CrazyGames reports **top-games data** for action games of approximately 13 minutes average play time and 8.1% D1 retention, versus 6.7% platform-wide D1. These are not the whole action genre, a live feed, or comparable to SPLATR's own yet-unmeasured cohorts. [CrazyGames action guidance](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/monetizing-action/)
- CrazyGames says strong Basic Launch titles often have 10+ minute average play time, 10–15% D1 retention and 80%+ of players playing at least one minute. Its “conversion” is a one-minute play threshold; Poki's conversion to play is reaching the first `gameplayStart()`. Do not compare these as the same metric. [CrazyGames](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/basic-launch-metrics/) · [Poki](https://developers.poki.com/guide/web-fit-test)
- Poki's player-fit test uses around 500 plays and calls 3+ minutes average plus 25% of plays over 3 minutes a healthy minimum for progression. Poki recommends 5+ minutes and 65%+ conversion to play before review; its stated platform average is around 6+ minutes and 70%. These are platform targets, not SPLATR data. [Poki testing](https://developers.poki.com/guide/how-testing-works)

No published real-player session, dropout, D1, sample size, or attack-readability results for SPLATR are available here. Metrics cannot reveal comprehension by themselves; observe players and ask what they thought happened.

## Live-game changes and limits

- On-screen enemy badges name the type and characteristic verb even in idle, prioritized by real attack states; charging shows an explicit state and a bar from the actual wind-up clock, not an invented countdown. They are screen-space, capped at four and positioned to avoid the player, each other and top HUD corners. Enemy body, direction and area telegraphs remain authoritative; the text does not change the simulation. Linked Prisms use their real beam wind-up. Off-screen warnings include a verb and actual charge progress where available.
- A real damaging hit now places a short **−1 HEALTH / remaining count / source** receipt near the cube and a persistent **LAST HIT** label near the score. A visibly distinct low-health HUD state appears at one health. Protected attempts and blocks must not produce or replace a damage receipt. The last-hit source is not a complete damage history. Receipt text and HUD work without motion or sound. Fatal hits still produce the actual cause in the result.
- The cues are intentionally bounded: some on-screen labels can be suppressed by overlap, edge notices are capped, and camera position never changes combat. A warning can still occur off-screen or be missed/obscured. The bar reflects simulation progress, not a promise of fixed wall-clock time; focus and gameplay pacing change it. No code can assert a player saw or understood it.
- Short words augment, not replace, the existing distinctive shapes, zone boundaries, colored effects, directional indicators, and live drills. Localization, visual crowding, high-speed overlaps, contrasting equipment/worlds and small screens need testing. Four labels and the 90-tick receipt are provisional, not empirically optimized.

## What to measure next

1. Recruit first-time players on touch and desktop. Without coaching, pause after their first hit and ask “what hit you, what warned you, what will you try next, how much health remains?” Record individual responses, device, build, exact encounter and sample size. Observe idle-to-warning recognition for all nine threats, including off-screen and overlapping attacks. Do not count prompting or simulator assertions as comprehension.
2. Watch the first run: record first input, first health loss/source, first low-health state, first death/source, retry, and whether each occurs before the player identifies the threat. Count the number of first-hit explanations correct divided by first-time players who had a first hit, with failures and nonresponses included. Compare the same device mix and build conditions before/after. Differentiate quits during a run from browser tab loss; timing is not proof of frustration.
3. When actually deployed with a suitable privacy-reviewed analytics setup, compare first-30-second and 3-minute survival/continued-play, first-run completion/retry and D1 per new-player cohort. Report numerator, denominator, version, period, source, definition, device, and uncertainty. Use randomized version assignment for causal comparisons and avoid selecting only retained players. Do not send personal identifiers or add network telemetry without a clear consent/privacy and platform plan. Current developer trace is local, bounded and lacks visibility timing and retention data.
4. Test every warning and hit at phone and desktop sizes, reduced motion, audio off, grayscale, every world, live blade effects and deliberately overlapping hazards. Observe whether the progress bar, real danger area, and remaining health are actually understood before tuning timing, attack frequency or health.
